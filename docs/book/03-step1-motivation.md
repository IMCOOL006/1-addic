# Ch 3 — Step 1: Strengthen Your Motivation and Commit to Change

Source: epub `index_split_010`. Part 2, Step 1. Paraphrased notes. Based on motivational interviewing and the transtheoretical model.

## Purpose

Explain why motivation swings, find your stage of change, rate your motivation, resolve ambivalence (decisional balance), and put your motivation into your own words. **Revisit any time**, even in maintenance.

## Key concepts

- **Motivation fluctuates** day to day, even hour to hour. This is true of any behavior change.
- **Stages of change** (transtheoretical model). You can move back and forth, at your own pace:
  1. **Precontemplation:** you don't see a need to change; others' concern feels like a burden.
  2. **Contemplation:** "maybe I need to change."
  3. **Preparation:** decided, and planning (appointment, treatment options, quit date, reading this book).
  4. **Action:** carrying out the plan (treatment, exercises, meetings, cut back or quit).
  5. **Maintenance:** 6+ months of consistent change (6 months sober).
  - A **slip doesn't send you back to the start.** Catching it early can keep you in Action.
- **Ambivalence is normal.** Substances did something for you at first, and those memories are powerful. The addicted brain uses them to tempt you.
- **Short-term vs. long-term effects.** The immediate benefits (better mood) are often the *opposite* of the delayed effects (depression, not getting things done). Thinking about the delayed negatives *before* deciding strengthens the rational brain.
- **Self-efficacy:** believing you can change improves your odds.
- **"What would move me up one point?"** is the recurring motivational question.

## Case story

- **Carrie:** smoked pot daily for 8 years and is functional, but anxiety, rumination (a breakup, a stalled career) and depressive lows are increasing. She is in contemplation, motivation 5/10. A psychologist walks her through four lenses (problem recognition, concern, intention, optimism). Afterward she rates herself 7/10.

## Exercises

### Ex 3.1 — What Stage of Change Are You In?
- **Type:** single select (5 stages) plus a 0–10 slider (anchors: Not, Somewhat, Motivated, Highly).
- **Interpretation:** below 5 suggests an earlier stage; 5 or above means some part of you has decided. Ask: *"What would it take to move up one point?"*
- **App mapping:** stage picker and motivation slider with a free-text answer to "+1 point." Store it **as a time series**, because this rating is meant to be re-taken (here, after Ex 3.3, and later in Ch 9's "Revisit Your Motivation").

### Ex 3.2 — Resolving Your Ambivalence (decisional balance matrix)
- **Type:** a 2×2 matrix of lists, at least 4 items per cell:
  - Benefits of reducing or quitting | Drawbacks of reducing or quitting
  - Benefits of continuing | Drawbacks of continuing
- **Example prompts (paraphrased) for each cell:**
  - *Why I use (benefits of continuing):* eases anxiety, relaxes me, an escape, fun, social ice-breaker.
  - *Negatives of using (drawbacks of continuing):* health problems, worse anxiety or depression, money problems, relationship damage, failing obligations.
  - *Benefits of quitting:* feel better physically, steadier emotions, more productive, better relationships (gradually), better self-esteem, save money.
  - *What I'll miss (drawbacks of quitting):* the immediate effects, distance from friends who use, fewer social or work activities feel comfortable, losing a coping crutch.
- **Follow-up action (important):** copy the **drawbacks of continuing** and **benefits of quitting** onto something you always carry (phone notes, phone wallpaper, a wallet card). **Look at it whenever a craving hits.**
- **App mapping:** a matrix editor with suggestion chips taken from the examples. Auto-generate a **Reasons Card** from two cells. It is pinned in the SOS/craving flow, can be exported as a lock-screen wallpaper image, and can show as a widget.

### Ex 3.3 — Motivation for Change (four lenses)
- **Type:** guided free-text prompts in four sections, then a re-rating on the 0–10 slider.
  1. **Is it a problem?** What problems has use caused? What has it stopped you from doing?
  2. **Concern:** What worries you? What will happen if you don't change?
  3. **Intention:** Why do you think you need to change? If it works out, how will your life be different?
  4. **Optimism / self-efficacy:** What makes you believe change is possible for you?
- **Interpretation:** compare with the earlier rating. A rise is great. No change is fine if you're being honest. Keep asking "+1?"
- **App mapping:** a stepper of four cards, each with Carrie's answers as optional examples, then a slider with a before/after delta animation. Feeds the Reasons Card ("my life will be different because…") and the "Future Me" vision.

## App hooks

- **Motivation meter:** a repeatable check-in with history. Weekly or on demand, and prompted after a lapse.
- **Reasons Card:** the most important artifact from this chapter. It's the first thing shown in the SOS flow.
- **The stage of change drives tone and content.** Precontemplation and contemplation users get gentler, exploratory content. Action users get skills and trackers. Maintenance users get a relapse-prevention focus.
- **Never reset progress to zero on a slip.** That's consistent with "a slip doesn't put you back at the beginning."
