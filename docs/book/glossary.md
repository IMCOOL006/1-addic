# Glossary of Book Concepts

Short definitions (paraphrased) for in-app copy, tooltips and the lesson outline. The chapter where each concept is introduced is in brackets.

| Term | Meaning | Ch |
|---|---|---|
| **Go mode / brakes** | Addiction impairs the brain's "stop" signal, so it gets stuck on "go." Recovery means re-engaging the brakes. | Intro, 7 |
| **Addicted brain** | The impulsive, conditioned part of you that tempts you and justifies using. It's the source of red flag thoughts. | 2 |
| **Rational brain** | The frontal-lobe part of you that weighs pros and cons and chooses. Practice makes it stronger. | 2 |
| **Positive reinforcement** | Using because it adds something good (pleasure, performance). | 1 |
| **Negative reinforcement / self-medicating** | Using to take away something bad (pain, anxiety, sadness). | 1 |
| **Tolerance** | Needing more for the same effect. A sign of moving toward addiction. | 1, 2 |
| **Withdrawal** | Unpleasant physical and emotional effects as the substance leaves your body. | 1, 5 |
| **Dopamine** | The brain's natural pleasure messenger. Drugs flood it, then deplete it. | 2 |
| **Conditioned cue / response** | Pavlov: people, places, things and emotions linked to use come to trigger cravings automatically. | 2 |
| **Stages of change** | Precontemplation → contemplation → preparation → action → maintenance (6+ months). You can move back and forth. | 3 |
| **Ambivalence** | Mixed feelings about changing. Normal at every stage. | 3 |
| **Decisional balance** | The 2×2 of benefits and drawbacks of quitting vs. continuing. | 3 |
| **Self-efficacy** | Believing you can succeed. It predicts success. | 3 |
| **"+1 point" question** | "What would it take to move my motivation up one point?" | 3 |
| **Paraphernalia** | The "stuff" used to drink or use. A powerful trigger; get rid of it. | 4 |
| **Be Smart, Not Strong** | Avoid triggers instead of testing your willpower. (Matrix Model slogan.) | 4 |
| **Seemingly irrelevant decisions (SIDs)** | Small choices that don't look like they're about using but move you toward relapse. | 4 |
| **Exit plan** | A pre-decided way out (and a person to call) if you choose a risky situation. | 4 |
| **Safety Net** | A person who supports your recovery and won't jeopardize it. | 4 |
| **Risky relationship** | Someone who uses around you or triggers conflict and negative emotions. Avoid, limit contact, or set limits. | 4 |
| **Passive / aggressive / assertive** | The three communication styles. Assertive = respectful, direct, uses "I" statements. | 4 |
| **"I feel… when…, and I need…"** | The assertive request formula. | 4 |
| **Recovery-interfering thoughts** | Beliefs that stop you asking for help ("I'm a burden," "I should be strong enough"). | 4 |
| **Mutual self-help groups** | AA, NA, MA, DRA (12-step); SMART Recovery (CBT-based). Social support is the active ingredient. | 4 |
| **Trigger** | Anything that starts the chain toward using. Internal (emotions, states) or external (people, places, situations, things). | 5 |
| **Trigger → Thought → Craving → Use** | The chain that CBT teaches you to notice and break. | 5 |
| **Red flag thought** | A thought that gives you "permission" to use (relapse justification). | 5 |
| **Six mistakes in thinking** | Black-or-white; discounting the positive; jumping to conclusions (mind reading, fortune telling); taking feelings too seriously; self-blame; labeling. | 5, 9 |
| **Lapse / slip** | The first use after a period of abstinence. Not yet a relapse. | 5, 10 |
| **Relapse** | A slip that continues into days or weeks of use. | 5, 10 |
| **Spiraling lapse** | Reading a slip as total failure, or as totally out of your control, which turns it into a relapse. | 5 |
| **Self-monitoring / metacognition** | Logging triggers, thoughts, feelings and cravings in real time; "thinking about thinking." | 5 |
| **Craving signature** | Where and how *you* feel cravings: body, emotions, thoughts. | 5 |
| **Three T's** | Triggering situation → (red flag) Thoughts → Thoughts on Trial (evidence → result → response). | 6 |
| **Superficial truth** | A short-term benefit that's real ("it does relax me") but leaves out what happens next. | 6 |
| **"Is it worth it?"** | The tiebreaker question when the evidence is mixed. | 6 |
| **Surf the urge** | A craving rises, peaks and falls like a wave, averaging about 15 minutes. Ride it out. | 6 |
| **Distract / Delay** | Shift attention early, and postpone the decision 15–20 minutes. Repeat if needed. | 6 |
| **High-risk situation** | Any situation likely to trigger you. Plan for it with a plan plus backups. | 6 |
| **Urge Planner** | A weekly plan for upcoming high-risk situations. | 6, 10 |
| **Mindfulness** | Paying attention on purpose, in the present, without judgment. | 7 |
| **Automatic pilot** | Doing things without awareness. Using often happens this way. | 7 |
| **Reacting vs. responding** | Automatic reflex vs. aware choice. | 7 |
| **SOBER breathing** | Stop · Observe · Breathe · Expand awareness · Respond mindfully (from MBRP). | 7 |
| **Acceptance** | Letting discomfort be there without letting it dictate your actions. The opposite of the addicted brain's "get rid of it." | 7 |
| **Self-compassion** | Meeting your own pain with kindness instead of criticism. | 7 |
| **Behavioral economics (rewards)** | Having many alternative rewards predicts staying sober. | 8 |
| **Behavioral activation** | Scheduling pleasant activities to lift mood and replace using. | 8 |
| **Pleasant activity** | Anything you rate 6 or higher (out of 10) for enjoyment. Aim for at least one a day. | 8 |
| **Should dos / want tos** | Responsibilities vs. things you do for fun. Balance them. | 8 |
| **Pleasure predicting** | Predict enjoyment (0–100%), do it anyway, rate the actual enjoyment, compare. | 8 |
| **Problem solving (3 steps)** | Brainstorm → weigh pros and cons → try the best one and evaluate. | 8 |
| **Dysfunctional thoughts** | Negative, depressive or anxious thoughts (not about using) that you challenge with the three T's. | 9 |
| **Give it a beat** | Pause before reacting in anger: self-talk, assertiveness, leave, breathe, count to 10, cry. | 9 |
| **Warning signs of relapse** | Shifts in mindset, behavior or emotion days or weeks before a relapse. | 10 |
| **Relapse prevention plan** | Your personal list of go-to skills, people and plans. | 10 |
