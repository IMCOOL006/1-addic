# Ch 6 — Step 4: Respond to Cognitive Distortions

Source: epub `index_split_013`. Part 2, Step 4. Paraphrased notes. The CBT *action* chapter: cognitive **and** behavioral coping. **The richest source for the SOS / craving toolkit.**

## Purpose

Build a repertoire of coping skills. **Cognitive:** challenge red flag thoughts and superficial truths. **Behavioral:** surf the urge, distract, delay, keep a schedule, plan for high-risk situations. Notice which ones work for *you*, because that feeds the Ch 10 plan.

## Key concepts

- **Outsmart your addicted brain.** Challenging red flag thoughts again and again makes the rational mindset "the new normal."
- **Be scientific:** (1) observe yourself, (2) question whether a thought is true and ask for evidence, (3) replace it with a balanced, evidence-based thought.
- **The three T's of thought challenging:**
  1. **Triggering situation**
  2. **(Red flag) Thoughts**
  3. **Thoughts on Trial:** examine the evidence with questions, then write the **Result** (is it rational?) and a **Response** (what to tell yourself to get back into the recovery mindset).
  - The tiebreaker question when the evidence is mixed: **"Is it worth it to use in this situation?"**
- **Superficial truths:** short-term benefits that *are* true ("it does feel good at first," "it eases anxiety") and that make using appealing. Put them in the bigger context: *"…but then what happens?"* (Same idea as "play the tape through" in other models.)
- **The internal-dialogue opener:** "Okay, wait a minute. I'm doing that black-or-white thinking again…"
- **Thinking vs. doing.** Some people (or the same person early in recovery, when cravings are overwhelming and concentration is poor) do better with **behavioral** strategies than cognitive ones. Both are valid. Find your mix.
- **Surf the urge.** The average craving lasts about **15 minutes**. Picture a **wave**: it rises, peaks, crashes and fades. Ride it without reacting. Mindfulness (Ch 7) extends this.
- **Distract yourself early.** Dwelling on the urge is like feeding a fantasy, and the more you imagine it, the more you want it. Distraction menu (paraphrased):
  - Picture a big red **STOP sign** and hold it.
  - **Rubber band** on the wrist; snap it to redirect attention.
  - **Leave** the triggering situation.
  - **Walk or exercise.**
  - An activity that holds you for **15–20 min** (write, read, go somewhere safe, call someone).
  - **Meditate** or relax (Ch 7).
  - **Pray.**
  - **Go to a meeting.** A meeting-finder app can locate one.
  - **Talk to someone** supportive, about the urge or about anything else.
  - **Imagery:** a safe, quiet place, or somewhere you'd love to be that isn't compatible with using.
  - Plus your own additions.
- **Delay the decision** for **15–20 minutes** using distractions. Once the urge passes, commit not to use. **If you're still unsure, repeat** (another 15–20 min).
- **Keep a schedule.** Plan the day ahead so you can see high-risk times coming. Mark triggering items with `**`. **Unstructured blocks** are vulnerable times, so fill them with recovery-compatible activities (meetings, sober friends).
- **Plan for high-risk situations.** Some can't be avoided (stressful coworkers or family, obligatory work events). For each, have **a plan plus a backup plan or two**.
- **Be patient with yourself.** These skills take practice, the same way the addiction took time to form.

## Case stories (worked examples of the three T's)

| Person | Craving | Trigger | Red flag thought | Key evidence | Response (paraphrased) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Scott** (alcohol) | 7/10 | Party with old drinking buddies | "Just one is no big deal; I can control it." | Can't remember the last time he stopped at one | "For me, one drink *is* a big deal; it has led straight to relapse." |
| **Lucy** (cocaine) | 8/10 | Depressed, low energy | "Only cocaine will make me feel better and get stuff done." | Feels better at first, then more depressed; crashes and gets nothing done for days; other things help | "Cocaine makes my depression worse, and I'm less productive afterward." |
| **Brian** (marijuana) | 7/10 | Going to a party alone, social anxiety | "If I'm high I won't feel self-conscious." | Less self-conscious, but distant and doesn't connect; low mood for 1–2 days | "Not worth it. I won't really connect, and I'll feel worse after." |

Scott's **schedule example** (Monday): wake 7, kids to school 8, work 9, **boss meeting 11:30\*\***, **lunch 12\*\*** (lunch was historically his first drink of the day), leave 5, dinner 6–7:30, **free time 7:30–9:30\*\*** (alone, kids asleep, wife on a late shift), time with wife 9:30 on.
- *Plan for the boss meeting and lunch:* lunch with a coworker (Bianca) he wouldn't drink with. *Backup:* bring lunch to his desk and schedule a call with his AA sponsor for that time.

## Responses to common thought errors (book table, paraphrased)

| Thought error | Red flag example | Rational response (gist) |
|---|---|---|
| Black-or-white | "Messed up everything at work, might as well drink" | Not *everything*; everyone makes mistakes; drinking makes it worse |
| Discounting the positive | "Boss was nice out of pity; nothing to lose" | One mistake doesn't cancel the positives; I have a lot to lose |
| Jumping to conclusions | "Treatment won't work for me; no point trying" | I can't know until I try and gather evidence; there are other options |
| Taking feelings too seriously | "I feel hopeless, so life is hopeless" | Feelings aren't facts and they change; working on myself changes things |
| Self-blame | "It's all my fault; I've ruined my life" | I have a disease and I'm taking positive steps; I can turn it around |
| Labeling | "I'm just a loser" | Having an illness doesn't make me a loser; with care I can recover |
| Spiraling lapse | "Already screwed up today, might as well go all out" | How I respond to a slip matters most; if I stop now I stay on the path; every decision counts |

## Exercises

### Ex 6.1 — Thought Challenging Worksheet
- **Fields:** triggering situation; red flag thoughts; thought on trial; **evidence the thought is accurate**; **evidence it's a superficial truth or inaccurate**; response (what I tell myself to get back into the recovery mindset).
- **App mapping:** the **"Put It On Trial"** guided flow, a courtroom metaphor. Evidence for and against appear as two columns; the **verdict** is rational or addicted-brain; the **closing statement** is the response. Pre-fill from the Red Flag library and Trigger Profile. Save responses to a personal **"Comebacks" deck** that shows up in SOS.

### Ex 6.2 — Challenging Your Red Flag Thoughts
- Same fields minus the trigger. It starts from a thought-error example the user wrote in Ch 5.
- **App mapping:** launches from any saved Ch 5 distortion pair. A one-tap "Challenge this thought."

### Ex 6.3 — Thought Challenging Form (daily log)
- **Columns:** triggering situation; red flag thoughts; thoughts on trial (questions I asked); alternative responses.
- **Guidance:** use it **daily, any time you're triggered**, extending the Ch 5 self-monitoring form. Review it with a counselor if you have one. (A downloadable form is at newharbinger.com/32783.)
- **App mapping:** an **optional step in the craving log**: after logging, "Want to challenge the thought?" leads to a compact three-T's flow.

### Ex 6.3b — Personal distraction list (unnumbered)
- **Type:** free-text list of the user's own distractions, added to the book's menu.
- **App mapping:** the **Distraction deck** in SOS. Each card is a book suggestion or a user-added one. Track which ones the user rates as helpful.

### Ex 6.4 — Daily Schedule
- **Type:** an hourly planner, 7:00 a.m. to 12:00 a.m., with `**` marking risky items. Also flag **empty or unstructured blocks**.
- **App mapping:** a **Day Planner** with hour blocks, a "risky" toggle per block, and auto-highlighted gaps. It suggests recovery-compatible fillers (from Ch 8's pleasant-activities list and Ch 4's exercise plan). Risky blocks prompt the Urge Planner.

### Ex 6.5 — Urge Planner
- **Type:** repeatable, up to 3 per week. **High-risk situation (day, time, situation)**, then **Plan**, then **Backup plan 1** and **Backup plan 2**.
- **App mapping:** a **"Battle Plan"** card attached to a calendar event, with a reminder before the event and a check-in after ("How did it go? Did you use the plan?"). Outcomes feed the Ch 10 plan and skill-effectiveness stats.

### Reflection prompt — cognitive vs. behavioral
- "Do cognitive or behavioral strategies help you more? Does it depend on the situation?"
- **App mapping:** a periodic reflection question. The app also infers this from the user's helpfulness ratings and shows it in the toolkit view.

## App hooks

- **SOS / Craving mode** (the app's most important feature) is assembled mostly from this chapter: **15-minute urge-surf timer** (wave animation) → **distraction deck** → **delay, then re-check** (repeat loop) → **call a Safety Net** → **Reasons Card** → **Comebacks**. The skill is "delay the decision," so the UX is literally a timer with a re-check at the end.
- **Skill effectiveness tracking:** after each use of a skill, a quick "did it help?" rating. That produces the data for "your go-to skills" (Ch 10).
- **Planner + Urge Planner = the Actions/Tasks backbone** for daily and weekly planning.
