# Ch 5 — Step 3: Become a "Self Expert"

Source: epub `index_split_012`. Part 2, Step 3. Paraphrased notes. The CBT *awareness* chapter: notice, don't fix yet. Fixing comes in Ch 6.

## Purpose

Know your addicted self inside out: your **triggers**, your **red flag thoughts** and thinking mistakes, how you **experience cravings**, and how to **self-monitor** daily. "Each time the rational brain wins, recovery gets stronger and the addicted brain loses power." Eventually healthy choices stop being a fight.

## Core model (the backbone of the app's tracker)

```
TRIGGER → THOUGHT → CRAVING → USE
```

- It often happens so fast that you only notice the craving at first. That's still a good start.
- Once you notice each link (physically, emotionally, cognitively), you can start an **inner dialogue** and choose not to use.

## Key concepts

- **Why urges happen.** *Physical:* dependence, then withdrawal, then "use to feel normal." *Psychological:* triggers conditioned to use. A psychological craving can be just as strong as a physical one, or stronger.
- **Trigger categories** (Marlatt & Gordon):
  - **Negative emotional states:** anxiety, sadness, boredom, loneliness, anger (self-medicating).
  - **Interpersonal conflict:** fights, rejection, criticism. It's *the conflict plus your emotional reaction to it*. More than 50% of relapses in a major study came from negative emotions stemming from interpersonal conflict.
  - **Social pressure:** *direct* (an offer) or *indirect* (being around people using). Can come from temptation or self-consciousness.
  - **Positive emotions:** celebrating, amplifying good moods. Find other sources of reward, and "think it all the way through."
- **Internal vs. external triggers.** Internal = emotions and states inside you. External = people, places, situations or activities, and things.
- **Thoughts drive coping.** How you think about a trigger decides how you cope with it. Irrational thoughts come mostly from the addicted brain and are often *automatic*, so you have to learn to **catch** them.
- **Red flag thoughts** (also called relapse-justifying thoughts) give you "permission" to use. Your job in this chapter is only to *notice* them.
- **Six mistakes in thinking.** These aren't about using directly, but they produce negative emotions, which produce red flag thoughts:
  1. **Black-or-white thinking:** all good or all bad; "nothing," "never"; perfectionism.
  2. **Discounting the positive:** a screen that lets only negatives through ("they said it out of pity").
  3. **Jumping to conclusions:** **mind reading** (assuming others think badly of you) or **fortune telling** (predicting doom).
  4. **Taking your feelings too seriously:** "I feel hopeless, so my life is hopeless."
  5. **Self-blame:** blaming yourself for what isn't in your control, including the addiction itself. *"Becoming an addict was not your choice; nor your fault."*
  6. **Labeling yourself:** "I feel X, so I am X" ("loser," "raging addict").
- **Spiraling lapse** (the abstinence violation effect). The **first use after abstinence is a lapse or slip, not a relapse.** What you tell yourself about it decides whether it becomes a relapse. There are two spiral interpretations:
  - **Personal failure:** guilt and shame, then using to numb them.
  - **Totally beyond my control:** you give up.
  - Typical spiral thoughts (paraphrased): one lapse means I'm a total failure; I've blown it so I might as well keep going; I'm hopeless; once an addict, always an addict; I'll never get back; I've screwed up today so I might as well go all out; I have no willpower; I'll always be addicted.
  - **Reframe:** relapse rates (40–60%) are similar to other chronic diseases (diabetes, hypertension, asthma). A lapse means **adjust the treatment, not that treatment failed**. In that moment, *"I didn't cope well in that situation."* A lapse is a **learning opportunity**.
- **Cravings have three components:** body, emotion, thought. Notice each one. Once you've broken it down, the outcome becomes a *decision*, not automatic.
- **Self-monitoring and metacognition:** "thinking about thinking." It's hard at first and gets easier with practice. Log in **real time** when possible.

## Case story

- **Scott (software engineer, alcohol, 2 months sober):** his boss writes him up over a client complaint. His thoughts: "He's picking on me / not fair," "I did nothing wrong," "Nothing I do is good enough," "I never succeed / I'll get fired," "Nobody likes me," "One drink would calm me down" (the red flag). ## Exercises

### Ex 5.1 — Identifying Triggers
- **Type:** a checklist with two marks per item: ✓ (have experienced) and ★ (comes up repeatedly). Plus "other" free text in each section.
- **Internal:** depression, loneliness, happiness, excitement, stress, irritability, feeling overwhelmed, jealousy, anxiety, boredom, anger, rejection, frustration, guilt or shame, needing an energy boost, withdrawal symptoms, other.
- **External:**
  - *People:* friends, spouse or partner, family, coworkers or boss, plus a named list.
  - *Places:* bars or clubs, friends' houses (named), concerts, school or work, neighborhoods or freeway exits, other.
  - *Situations or activities:* parties or meeting new people, holidays or special occasions, home alone, dining out, before or during a date, waking up, after work or school, other.
  - *Things:* stashes, paraphernalia (favorite glasses, bongs, papers, pipes, ritual objects), other.
- **App mapping:** a **Trigger Profile** (personal trigger library), each with a status of *experienced* or *frequent ★*. It powers the tracker's quick-pick chips, the high-risk planner (Ch 6), and analytics ("your top triggers this month"). Starred triggers become "bosses" or priority targets in the game layer.

### Ex 5.2 — Identifying Red Flag Thoughts
- **Type:** checklist plus "other."
- **Items (paraphrased from Brown et al. 2006):** "I have to…"; "Nobody has to know"; "It doesn't matter if…"; "I can control it"; "Just one"; "Bad day, might as well"; "I deserve to…"; "Special occasion, I'll start over tomorrow."
- **App mapping:** a personal **Red Flag library**. These are the "addicted brain's lines" in the game layer. Each one gets a rebuttal in Ch 6.

### Ex 5.2b — Mistakes in Thinking (six mini-exercises, unnumbered in the book)
- **Type:** for each of the six distortions, a pair of free-text fields: *my example of the distorted thought* → *the red flag thought it leads to*. Skip any that don't fit you.
- **Book examples (paraphrased):** "I messed up everything at work" → "might as well drink"; "Boss was nice out of pity" → "I'm pathetic, nothing to lose"; "Brother blew me off, he thinks he's better" → "I'll show him, I'll use"; "Treatment won't work for me" → "no point trying"; "I feel hopeless so life is hopeless" → "using means not feeling the pain"; "It's all my fault" → "I've ruined my life, nothing to lose"; "I relapsed, I'm a raging addict" → "no point staying clean."
- **App mapping:** a **"Distortion Spotter"** lesson with a sort-the-thought mini-game (sort thoughts into the 6 categories), then the personal pair-writing. Tag each saved thought with its distortion type so Ch 6's challenge tools can pull them up.

### Ex 5.2c — Spiraling Lapse thoughts (unnumbered)
- **Type:** checklist of spiral thoughts plus free text "others I've had."
- **App mapping:** feeds the **Lapse Recovery flow** (see Ch 10). It shows the user's own spiral thoughts alongside reframes.

### Ex 5.3 — How Do You Experience Cravings?
- **Type:** three multi-select sections, each with "other."
  - *Body:* chest tightness, stomach, jaw, neck, shoulders, racing heart, nose (smelling the substance), other.
  - *Emotions:* anxiety, excitement, anticipation, restlessness, irritability, "the same emotions as when drinking or high," other.
  - *Thoughts:* "I need it," "I have to have…," "I'll go crazy if I don't," "I can't handle…," "I can't get it out of my head," other.
- **App mapping:** a **Craving Signature** profile. Use a **body map** UI (tap regions). The signature preloads into the craving log and the urge-surf script (Ch 6) so the user can practice "noticing" in their own terms.

### Ex 5.4 — Craving Self-Monitoring Form (daily log)
- **Type:** a repeating log row: **date/time and triggering situation (internal or external)**; **thoughts**; **feelings**; **craving intensity 0–10** (0 none, 5 average and manageable, 10 severe and constant).
- **Guidance:** fill it out **daily**, in **real time** where possible. Over time, look for patterns you weren't aware of. (The publisher offers a downloadable form at newharbinger.com/32783.)
- **App mapping:** **the core tracker entry.** A fast "Log a craving" flow: time (auto), trigger chips from the Trigger Profile, thought (free text plus red-flag chips), feelings chips, 0–10 slider. Optional: what I did, and the outcome (Ch 6 adds coping). Feeds pattern insights (time of day, trigger, intensity trend).

## App hooks

- **"Self Expert" is a skill tree node.** It's about awareness, not success. Reward *noticing and logging*, not only abstaining.
- **The chain diagram (Trigger → Thought → Craving → Use)** is a reusable visual. In the tracker, each log shows the user's chain, and Ch 6 adds a "break the chain" link.
- The **spiraling lapse** concept is critical for gamification ethics. **Never use streak-reset shame.** Use "learning opportunity" framing, and keep cumulative stats ("total sober days," "cravings surfed") that never reset.
