# Ch 7 — Step 5: Become Mindful

Source: epub `index_split_014`. Part 2, Step 5. Paraphrased notes. Based on mindfulness-based relapse prevention (MBRP: Bowen, Chawla & Marlatt) and Kabat-Zinn's MBSR.

## Purpose

Add an **acceptance-based** skill set alongside CBT. When a craving is too strong to "think through," you can **observe and tolerate** it without acting on it. The chapter covers what mindfulness is, why it helps recovery, and practice exercises, ending with **SOBER breathing**, the in-the-moment craving skill.

## Key concepts

- **Definition** (Kabat-Zinn, paraphrased): awareness that comes from paying attention **on purpose**, **in the present moment**, and **non-judgmentally** to experience as it unfolds. The book breaks it into four parts:
  - **Awareness** of habitual sequences (sensations, breath, thoughts, intentions, emotions) *before* using happens.
  - **Paying attention on purpose, now:** notice when the mind wanders and bring it back.
  - **Non-judgmentally**, with **gentle kindness** toward yourself. Drop "I shouldn't feel this."
  - **Moment by moment:** experiences are temporary. Watch them change with **open curiosity**.
- **Why it helps recovery:** it manages **stress** (a top relapse trigger); it retrains a brain damaged by heavy use toward **thoughtful responses** instead of quick fixes; it reduces **depression and anxiety**; it **reduces cravings** and **prevents relapse** (MBRP trials).
- **Automatic pilot:** doing routine things (brushing teeth, driving, showering) while the mind is in the past or future. Addictive use often happens on autopilot. Mindfulness shifts you from **reacting** to **choosing**.
- **No right or wrong way** to practice. Awkwardness is normal. Every time you notice your mind wandering, **congratulate yourself** and come back.
- **These are not relaxation exercises.** They're observation exercises. Whatever you feel (pleasant, neutral, uncomfortable) is fine.
- **A useful phrase:** "This is just the way things are right now."
- **Reacting vs. responding.** Reacting is automatic and intuitive, like pulling back from fire. But an addicted intuition "protects" you by using. Responding means observe first, then choose with awareness and weigh the consequences.
- **Acceptance** is the opposite of what the addicted brain says ("Go, get rid of the craving / the sadness / don't let this good feeling end"). Trying to **escape discomfort makes it stronger**. Accept that it's there while recognizing it doesn't have to dictate what you do.
  - Acceptance self-talk (paraphrased): it's okay to see things as they are, not as they should be; accepting a feeling doesn't mean I'm satisfied or giving up on change; once I see things clearly, I can find the energy to heal and change.
- **Kindness and compassion.** Kindness is a friendly, welcoming attitude toward whatever comes up, including urges during practice. Compassion is a tender response to your own pain. It quiets the self-critical inner voice, which can itself drive use.

## Case story

- **Hillary (34, alcohol and cocaine):** she was nearly 3 months sober and caring for her mother through cancer. After her mother died she relapsed to escape the grief. She restarted recovery 2 weeks ago, and grief brings urges. She uses **SOBER** during waves of sadness and tells herself, "It's okay to feel this; I can tolerate it; escaping doesn't help." Over time the sadness becomes bearable without using.

## Exercises (all are guided practices, which suits audio)

### Ex 7.1 — Observe Your Body (body scan)
- **Script outline:** sit and notice your breath (about 1 min) → points of contact (chair, floor) → feet (temperature, shoes, toes, sole, ankle) → legs (calves, knees, thighs, contact) → hips and waist (let pain be as it is) → lower and upper back → hands up to the shoulders → neck → face (forehead, eyelids, brows, nose, cheeks) → lips, jaw, chin → top of the head → the whole body → back to the breath.
- **App mapping:** a **guided audio and animated body scan** (about 8–12 min), written as an **original script** that follows this structure. An optional post-practice note.

### Ex 7.2 — Bringing Mindfulness into Daily Life
- Three informal practices, each with a reflection ("What did you notice? Any sensations you hadn't noticed before?"):
  - **Mindful shower:** step in slowly, the dial, water on skin, temperature, sound, smell, then carry it into getting ready.
  - **Mindful eating:** a piece of fruit. Look at it as if for the first time, hold it, listen, smell, notice salivation, rest it in the mouth, chew (flavor and texture), notice the intention to swallow, swallow. Optionally reflect on where the fruit came from (interconnection).
  - **Mindful walking:** stand and feel your feet and weight → shift weight → lift, move and place each foot, heel first → a few minutes. Ideally someone reads you the instructions, *which is ideal for audio guidance*.
- **App mapping:** **"Everyday Mindfulness" micro-quests** (3 practices), each a short guide with a reflection journal entry. Can be scheduled as daily habits.

### Ex 7.3 — Breathing with Awareness
- **Script outline:** sit and notice contact → breath through the nose into the lungs → belly rising and falling (hand on abdomen optional) → temperature of inhale vs. exhale → let the breath be natural → notice thoughts and come back.
- **Duration:** start around **5 min** and build up to **30 min**.
- **App mapping:** a **breath practice** with a progressive duration ladder (5 → 10 → 15 → 20 → 30 min) and a simple breathing visual. *Don't* pace the breath. The book says not to change it, so the visual should be ambient, not an inhale/exhale pacer.

### Ex 7.4 — SOBER Breathing ⭐ (the in-the-moment craving skill)
- **Acronym** (Bowen, Chawla & Marlatt 2010):
  - **S**top: step out of autopilot and interrupt the automatic sequence.
  - **O**bserve: what's happening right now? Body sensations, thoughts about using, emotions. Allow them.
  - **B**reathe: bring attention to the breath.
  - **E**xpand awareness to the whole body.
  - **R**espond mindfully: you can accept the discomfort *and* choose, with awareness, an action that's self-protective and fits your recovery goals.
- **Takes 1–2 minutes.** It's a "go-to" skill.
- **Practice progression** (each with an observations note, plus "Was it helpful?"):
  1. **Imagined** tempting situation, then imagine making the best choice and notice how that feels.
  2. When **stressed**.
  3. During an **unpleasant emotion** (sadness, anxiety, anger…).
  4. During a real **temptation or craving**.
- **App mapping:** a **SOBER card in SOS** (a 2-minute guided flow with 5 steps). Plus a **4-stage practice quest** (imagined → stress → emotion → craving), each with a reflection and a helpfulness rating. The "Respond" step hands off to the user's options (Safety Net call, distraction deck, leave the situation).
