# Ch 10 — Your Personalized Recovery Plan

Source: epub `index_split_017`. Part 2, the capstone. Paraphrased notes. *The book labels its one exercise here "Exercise 11.1." That's a typo; we call it Ex 10.1.*

## Purpose

Bring everything together for the long term: (1) **stay one step ahead** with weekly planning, (2) know your **warning signs** and have a plan for each, (3) know how to **stop a slip** before it becomes a relapse, and (4) build your **personalized relapse prevention plan** from the skills that actually helped you.

## Key concepts

- **A weekly look-ahead** for **at least the first 6 months**. At the start of each week, list risky or stressful situations coming up (family, work…) and plan for each: leave if triggered, keep the visit short, meditate, bring a sober person, or avoid it altogether. This reuses the **Urge Planner (Ex 6.5)**. Discuss it with a counselor if you have one.
- **Recovery is a mindset as well as behaviors.** Warning signs show up in thinking, behavior and emotion, often days or **weeks before** a relapse.
- **Slip vs. relapse.** A **slip** is the first use after a period of abstinence. A **relapse** is a slip that continues into days or weeks of use. Returning to use is "part of the illness" (NIDA), so prepare for it.
- **Three steps right after a slip:** (1) **talk** about it (counselor, therapist, sponsor), (2) **figure out what went wrong**, (3) **put safeguards in place**.
- **Self-talk after a slip** (paraphrased): it's behind me as long as I don't repeat it; it's a learning opportunity; I'll feel bad, but that passes, and I'll feel good about myself again once I'm back on track.
- **Watch for the spiraling lapse** ("I've blown it, total failure, nothing to lose"). Recognize it and deliberately shift to a CBT, learning-oriented mindset.
- **After you've stabilized:**
  1. **Revisit motivation** (Ex 3.2, 3.3).
  2. **Learn from it.** Ideally with a therapist, review: Were there warning signs (days, weeks, months before)? Did you address them? If so, what more could you have done? If not, why not? How did you end up in that situation? What was the trigger? How did it affect you? How can you cope better next time?
  3. **Step up support.** A slip means part of the plan wasn't firmly in place. Options: more self-help groups, more or more frequent therapy, more contact with sober people, consider (or adjust) medication for the addiction or for mental health.
- **Two kinds of skills:**
  - **General recovery lifestyle skills** keep you balanced and lower your baseline risk (pleasant activities, exercise, communication).
  - **Situation-specific coping skills** are for risky moments (urge surfing, SOBER, challenging red flags).
  - **Pick the ones that "spoke to you,"** especially ones you found yourself **using in real life** without the book.

## Exercises

### Ex 10.1 — My Warning Signs of Relapse (book: "11.1")
- **Type:** checklist plus "other," then pick the **top 3**, each with a **plan if I notice this sign**.
- **Items (paraphrased):**
  - Stopping or cutting back therapy or counseling
  - Thinking about how to use without anyone knowing
  - Often fantasizing about using while ignoring the consequences
  - Stopping or cutting back self-help groups (AA, SMART)
  - Isolating
  - Putting yourself in risky situations
  - Avoiding talking about doubts or mixed feelings about sobriety
  - Starting to use a drug other than your main one
  - Intense negative emotions (depression, anxiety, anger, irritability)
  - Feeling you don't fit in with others in recovery
  - Blaming others for your problems
  - Very few fun activities in your routine
  - Poor or too little sleep
  - Neglecting responsibilities (bills, chores, caregiving, work or school)
  - Avoiding talking about unhappiness
  - Feeling hopeless about rebuilding your life
  - Keeping alcohol, drugs or paraphernalia at home
  - Keeping dealer or using-friend phone numbers
  - Lying
  - Rejecting help
  - Boredom or lots of unstructured time
  - Spending time with people who use
  - Getting defensive when others express concern
- **Example plans** (paraphrased): talk to your therapist; start or resume therapy; see a psychiatrist about medication for mood or anxiety; go to more meetings; call your sponsor or find one; redo the workbook exercises on motivation, thought challenging and pleasant activities; start or step up exercise.
- **App mapping:**
  - **The Warning Sign Radar:** the user's top 3 signs, each with a plan.
  - **Automatic detection where possible from in-app data:** missed meetings or therapy logs, fewer pleasant activities (Ex 8.1), poor-sleep check-ins, rising negative-emotion logs, fewer Safety Net contacts, more unstructured time in the planner. When a sign trips, gently show **the user's own plan** for it.
  - **Optional "trusted person" check:** a Safety Net can be given a read-only list of your warning signs, so "someone close to you points them out" (the book's phrase) is easier to act on. This is opt-in and later-phase.

### Ex 10.2 — Slip Review (unnumbered; built from the "Learn from the situation" questions)
- **Fields:** warning signs beforehand; did I address them (what more, or why not); how I ended up in the situation; the trigger; how it affected me; how to cope better next time; support to add.
- **App mapping:** the **Lapse Recovery flow**. Entry: "I slipped." The flow is: compassionate message (no shame, no streak wipe) → spiraling-lapse self-talk check (shows the user's own spiral thoughts from Ch 5 with rebuttals) → immediate safety (stop now; contact a Safety Net, counselor or sponsor) → motivation revisit (Reasons Card, slider) → **Slip Review** → "add support" actions → updated plan. Shareable with a therapist (export).

### Ex 10.3 — Personalized Relapse Prevention Plan (unnumbered)
- **Type:** review a checklist of every skill in the book, tick the helpful ones, then write your go-to list:
  - Motivation techniques (3)
  - General wellness skills (6)
  - Alcohol- and drug-specific coping skills (6)
  - **People I can call** (3)
  - Mood and anger techniques (3)
  - Other skills (3)
- **Skill checklist, by category:**
  - **Motivation enhancing:** stage of change and 0–10 rating and "+1"; benefits/drawbacks matrix; review problems use caused; concerns and imagined future; how quitting affects my life; reasons I believe I can do it.
  - **General wellness:** awareness of SIDs; choosing and committing to exercise; exercise log; rewards for exercise; Safety Nets vs. risky relationships; assertive requests and refusals; expanding my network; keeping a schedule; scheduling non-using pleasant activities; rewards for pleasant activities.
  - **Alcohol- and drug-specific:** identify triggers; know my red flag thoughts; spot and challenge thinking mistakes; monitor triggers and cravings; remove all substances and paraphernalia; urge surfing; distraction; delay 15 min; Urge Planner; mindfulness practice; SOBER for cravings; mindful acceptance; involvement in mutual-help groups.
  - **Mood and anger:** mood and craving monitoring; anxiety and craving monitoring; challenge anxious or depressive thoughts; anger triggers; "give it a beat"; challenge angry thoughts.
- **App mapping:** **"My Recovery Plan,"** a living document the app **pre-fills** from the user's actual data: skills ranked by the helpfulness ratings they gave after each use, Safety Nets from the network map, top triggers, top warning signs and plans, the Reasons Card. The user edits and confirms. It's exportable (PDF or print) and always reachable from SOS. **This is the "final boss" or graduation artifact** of the learning path, but it stays editable forever.

## Foreword (Richard Rawson): design principles we should adopt

Rawson lists four principles for effective health materials, and they work as app design principles too:
1. **Educate** with scientifically accurate information.
2. **Empower** people to act and take part in their own health.
3. Be **respectful**: not preachy, judgmental or condescending. Treat readers as responsible adults.
4. Make it **relatable, interesting and realistic**, using case examples and conversation scripts.

He also notes the book draws on **MI, CBT, community reinforcement, relapse prevention, contingency management, and the Matrix Model**, plus promising approaches (behavioral activation, mindfulness, DBT, exercise). "There is no 'right way' to recovery."
