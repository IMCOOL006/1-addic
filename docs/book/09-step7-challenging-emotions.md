# Ch 9 — Step 7: Conquer Challenging Emotions

Source: epub `index_split_016`. Part 2, Step 7. Paraphrased notes. Applies the existing toolkit (CBT, MI, BA, mindfulness) to **depression, anxiety and anger**.

## Purpose

Negative emotions are among the most common relapse triggers. Learn to anticipate them (emotional triggers), monitor mood and anxiety alongside cravings, challenge depressive or anxious thoughts with the three T's, manage anger ("give it a beat"), cope with any negative emotion quickly, and revisit motivation.

## Key concepts

- **Co-occurring conditions are common.** Up to **two-thirds** of people with addictions have mental health issues, most often anxiety and mood disorders. Substances were a "Band-Aid," and over time they make depression and anxiety **worse**.
- **Four approaches for negative moods** (already learned): (1) monitor and challenge the thoughts, (2) revisit motivation, (3) pleasant activities and behavioral coping, (4) mindfulness.
- **Know your emotional triggers:** review the internal triggers from Ex 5.1 and try to catch them *before* they're full-blown.
- **Early-recovery low mood** is often withdrawal-related and may lift after about a month. **If several depression symptoms persist beyond a month sober, see a professional.** Untreated depression lowers the odds of recovery.
- **Depression symptoms** (book list, paraphrased): sadness; can't enjoy things; crying spells; low energy; restlessness; sleep problems (too little or too much); appetite changes; loss of energy; poor concentration; trouble with everyday decisions; worthlessness; hopelessness; **thoughts of death or suicide**.
  - ⚠️ *App safety requirement:* any time the user reports suicidal thoughts or severe hopelessness, show crisis resources immediately (e.g., 988 in the US, plus local equivalents). See Plan → Safety.
- **The same six distortions fuel depression and anxiety.** Examples (paraphrased): date canceled → "always alone" (black-or-white); compliment → "she pities me" (discounting); "I won't get the job" (fortune telling); "I can't enjoy anything, so I'll be miserable forever" (feelings as facts); "my depression is my own fault" (self-blame); "only losers need treatment" (labeling).
- **Anxiety types that commonly co-occur** (brief psychoeducation; the app must not diagnose):
  - **Generalized anxiety:** chronic hard-to-stop worry, plus fatigue, tension, irritability, nausea.
  - **Social anxiety:** fear of judgment in social or performance situations, with physical symptoms.
  - **Panic attacks / panic disorder:** sudden intense fear, racing heart, shortness of breath, fear of dying or losing control; then fear of the attacks themselves.
  - **PTSD:** after trauma; numbness, nightmares, flashbacks, hopelessness, disconnection, avoidance.
  - Get evaluated if any of these fit. Anxiety can **temporarily worsen in early recovery** as suppressed emotions come back.
- **Anger.** Losing your temper makes you look out of control, people stop taking you seriously, your needs go unmet, and you lose self-respect. **Anger control is about controlling the response, not preventing the feeling.**
- **Anger trigger categories:** people, places (the car), feelings (rejected, ignored, impatient), thoughts ("why is this line so slow?"), physical sensations (withdrawal irritability).
- **"Give it a beat"** strategies:
  - **Inner dialogue** (paraphrased): "I can keep my cool"; "it's up to me to stay in control"; "I can get through this in a way I'll feel good about"; "I can only control my own behavior."
  - **Be assertive:** "I'm upset and need time to gather my thoughts."
  - **Leave** the situation.
  - **Focus on your breath.**
  - **Count to ten.**
  - **Allow yourself to cry.**
  - Then challenge the angry thoughts and use assertive communication (Ch 4).
- **A quick coping menu for any negative emotion** (anger, sadness, guilt, shame, anxiety):
  - SOBER breathing (about 5 min)
  - Relax: breath, walk, bath, music
  - A pleasant activity from Ex 8.3
  - Exercise
  - Call someone, even without talking about the problem
  - Go to a meeting
  - **Reassuring self-talk** (paraphrased): "I can get through this," "I can handle this," "I can cope with whatever comes," "one day, one moment at a time," "I don't have to act on this feeling right now"
  - **Give yourself credit** for getting through it without using
  - **Mindful acceptance** (paraphrased): "This is how it is right now," "I don't like it, but I can tolerate it," "I won't judge this feeling; maybe I can learn from it"
- **Revisit motivation:** reread the Ex 3.2 matrix (especially "relief from bad feelings" as a benefit of using vs. the drawbacks of continuing and benefits of quitting), rate motivation 0–10, and ask "+1?"

## Case stories

- **Alicia (alcohol, about 1 month sober):** invites Vince (new, also sober) to a work party at a bar. He cancels for work and offers dinner instead. Thoughts: "nothing going for me / he pities me / always alone." Using the three T's she finds: he accepted enthusiastically, he initiated their past dates, he offered an alternative; she's sober, has a job and family; "always alone" is fortune telling. Distortions identified: discounting the positive, black-or-white, jumping to conclusions.
- **Jenny (alcohol, 6 weeks sober):** her husband refuses to take their son Sammy to basketball so she can see friends, and leaves. She sends a screaming voicemail. Sammy asks if she doesn't like going with him. Guilt and shame follow, then a strong craving. Alternative responses: it's understandable to be angry but don't hang on to it; plan weekends together later when calm; make the best of it (ice cream with Sammy); apologize to Sammy and model healthy anger; 5–10 min of mindful breathing.

## Exercises

### Ex 9.1 — Mood and Craving Log
- **Columns:** date; situation; thought; **depression rating** (the text says 1–10, the column header says 0–10; use **0–10** for consistency); **craving rating 0–10** (overall for the day).
- **Guidance:** **at least a few weeks.** Look for links (more depressing thoughts → stronger cravings? themes?). (Downloadable at newharbinger.com/32783.)
- **App mapping:** an **emotion entry** type in the unified journal, with emotion = *depressed*, an intensity slider, situation and thought fields, and a same-day craving rating. Charts correlate mood and craving.

### Ex 9.2 — Anxiety and Craving Log
- **Columns:** date; situation; thought; **anxiety rating 0–10**; **craving rating 0–10**.
- **Guidance:** look for patterns and themes. Ask "Is anxiety or depression the bigger issue for me?"
- **App mapping:** the same entry type with emotion = *anxious*. **Design note: Ex 5.4, 6.3, 8.1, 9.1 and 9.2 should all be one flexible "check-in / log" model** with optional sections, not five separate forms.

### Ex 9.3 — Challenging Your Negative Thoughts
- **Type:** 2 situations, each with: situation; negative thoughts **and the emotions they led to**; **thought error identified**; thoughts on trial; evidence for; evidence against; response ("see the situation more realistically").
- **App mapping:** **reuse the "Put It On Trial" flow** from Ex 6.1 with a "dysfunctional thought" mode (no red-flag/using framing). Add a distortion-type picker.

### Ex 9.3b — "When I am angry" self-profile (unnumbered)
- **Fields:** physical sensations; frequent thoughts; how I express it when I can't control it.
- **App mapping:** an **Anger Signature**, a sibling of the Craving Signature (Ex 5.3), reusing the body map UI.

### Ex 9.4 — Anger Triggers
- **Type:** free text across 5 categories: people, places, thoughts, feelings, physical sensations.
- **App mapping:** extends the Trigger Profile with an "anger" tag, so the same library holds craving triggers and anger triggers.

### Ex 9.5 — Changing Angry Behavior
- **Fields:** when I felt angry: the trigger; my thoughts; my unhealthy responses; alternative, healthier responses.
- **App mapping:** a **"Replay & Rewrite"** flow: reflect on a past incident and script better responses. Saved alternatives join the user's "Give it a beat" deck.

### Ex 9.6 — Revisit Your Motivation (unnumbered)
- **Type:** reread the Ex 3.2 matrix, then the 0–10 motivation slider, then "+1?"
- **App mapping:** **the same Motivation Meter entry** as Ex 3.1/3.3 (shared time series). Suggest it automatically after high-intensity negative-emotion logs.

## App hooks

- **An "I'm feeling…" entry point** next to "I'm craving…" in SOS. It routes to anger tools (give it a beat, count to ten, leave, breathe), sadness and anxiety tools (SOBER, reassuring phrases, acceptance, call someone), and always ends with a motivation check.
- **Safety gating:** the depression-symptom content and any self-reported suicidal thoughts trigger crisis resources. The "persistent symptoms > 1 month → see a professional" rule fits a gentle, dismissible nudge driven by mood-log trends.
- **Gamification note:** don't reward high mood scores. Reward *logging honestly and using a skill*.
