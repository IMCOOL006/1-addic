# Ch 4 — Step 2: Set Yourself Up to Succeed

Source: epub `index_split_011`. Part 2, Step 2. Paraphrased notes. The largest "practical setup" chapter.

## Purpose

Prepare your environment and relationships for early recovery: clear out paraphernalia, notice seemingly irrelevant decisions, start exercising, map your social support, communicate assertively, refuse offers, and grow your network (including mutual-help groups). The book says to pace yourself, because you don't have to do it all at once.

## Key concepts

- **Get rid of "stuff."** Paraphernalia and the substance itself are powerful conditioned triggers. Remove them from home, bag, car and phone.
  - Common objections, each with a recovery-mindset reframe:
    - "It cost money." → Using costs far more. Build barriers to relapse.
    - "What about guests who drink?" → My home is alcohol-free. Socialize at restaurants instead.
    - "I should be strong enough." → **"Be Smart, Not Strong!"** (a slogan from the Matrix Model / Rawson). Visual cues seen for **33 ms** can activate craving circuits. Avoid triggers rather than test yourself.
- **Seemingly irrelevant decisions (SIDs).** Small everyday choices that don't *look* like they involve using but move you toward relapse. A substance that isn't your drug of choice can also lead back to it.
  - How to handle SIDs: (1) be aware that moment-to-moment choices matter, (2) consider all your options, (3) weigh the risks and benefits of each, (4) pick the most protective one, (5) if you choose a risky one, have an **exit plan** (call a sponsor, bring a supportive person).
- **Exercise helps recovery.** CDC guidance is 150 min/week of moderate aerobic activity (about 30 min × 5 days) plus strength training 2×/week. Studies in recovery show better fitness and mood, less use, and *more dopamine availability after about 2 months*. Choose something **enjoyable**, not a chore. **Reward yourself** and link the reward explicitly to the exercise.
- **Social support** strongly predicts staying sober. Ways to adjust your network: new friends in recovery, cutting back on triggering friends, leaning on the helpful ones, teaching willing people how to help, family or couples therapy.
  - **Safety Nets** are people who support your recovery and won't jeopardize it. **Risky relationships** are people who use around you, or who are a source of conflict and negative emotions that lead to urges.
  - Ways to handle risky people: **Avoid** (dealers, friends whose bond was all about using), **Limit contact** (coworkers you used with), **Set limits** (family, partner, close friends).
- **Communication styles.** *Passive:* keeps feelings and needs inside, which leads to resentment and depression. *Aggressive:* demanding, hostile, which isolates you. *Assertive:* respectful, makes requests without demanding, thanks others, expresses feelings in a healthy way.
- **Recovery-interfering thoughts** stop you from asking for help: "I don't want to burden them," "they'll leave if they have to change," "I should be strong enough." Each gets an assertive reframe.
- **Listening skills.** *Verbal:* ask clarifying questions, paraphrase ("If I heard you right…"), use "I" statements. *Nonverbal:* eye contact, nodding, leaning in, phone away.
- **The "I feel ___ when ___, and I need ___" formula.** Rick's model request starts with appreciation, uses "I" statements, states his own concern, makes a direct request, and stays polite.
  - Other uses: ask loved ones not to keep bringing up past hurts; ask for patience with your mood swings or need for time alone; explain which behaviors trigger you (arguing, criticism); invite a loved one to counseling.
- **Saying no to offers.** Social pressure causes about **1 in 3** slips. The script: a firm "No, thank you" with **eye contact**; **ask for something else** ("Do you have sparkling water?") or suggest a different activity; **change the subject**; **ask them to stop offering** ("I've stopped drinking"); **self-talk** about why your recovery matters. **Role-play** with a friend or sponsor.
- **Mutual self-help groups** (AA, NA, Marijuana Anonymous, Dual Recovery Anonymous; SMART Recovery as a CBT-based, non-spiritual alternative). Not for everyone. The social support is the active ingredient. You don't have to agree with everything. Try many meetings and formats (speaker, step study). **Get involved**: a sponsor, a "commitment" (setting up chairs or coffee). More involvement predicts better outcomes.

## Case stories

- **Jonathan (cocaine):** a Super Bowl win, then a karaoke bar (the SID), then a friend has a beer waiting, then several beers, then cocaine is offered, then he relapses. The chain traces back to the decision to go to the bar.
- **Rick (alcohol):** recently sober; his girlfriend Tanya drinks wine and hosts parties. He stays passive and says nothing, then drinks secretly after a party (5 drinks). This illustrates recovery-interfering thoughts and the assertive request script.

## Exercises

### Ex 4.1 — Paraphernalia
- **Type:** checklist plus "other" free text, then a plan.
- **Items:** alcohol; pills; marijuana; any other drugs used (even if not your main one); lighters; mirrors; ashtrays; pipes; rolling papers; bongs; needles; **dealer and contact phone numbers (delete them)**; medical marijuana card; other.
- **Follow-up:** decide whether you'll do it alone or with someone (a loved one, sponsor or counselor). **Don't procrastinate.**
- **App mapping:** a **"Clear the Space" quest**. Each checked item becomes a sub-task to tick off when it's gone, with an optional "who's helping me" contact. Show a safety note for needles and syringes (dispose safely) and for medications (take-back programs).

### Ex 4.2 — Selecting and Committing to an Exercise Activity
- **Type:** form: up to 5 enjoyable activities; "I'll try ___ on [date]"; a backup activity with a date; "___ minutes, ___ days/week."
- **App mapping:** an activity picker, then calendar commitments (try-dates as scheduled tasks), then a weekly target. Feeds the Exercise Log and the Ch 6 schedule.

### Ex 4.3 — Exercise Log
- **Type:** a weekly log, Mon–Sun: exercise type and minutes, plus the reward given. End of week: met my goal Y/N; rewarded myself at least once Y/N.
- **Guidance:** reward yourself at least 1–2× a week. Use the log consistently for several months.
- **App mapping:** a tracker with a weekly-goal ring, plus **user-defined rewards**. The game's reward system should hand out the user's *own* chosen real-life rewards, matching the book's advice.

### Ex 4.4 — Safety Nets vs. Risky Relationships
- **Type:** two lists, up to 6 names each.
- **Follow-up:** do I have at least a few solid Safety Nets? For each risky person, pick a strategy: Avoid, Limit contact, or Set limits.
- **App mapping:** a **support network map** (contacts with a role tag: Safety Net or Risky; a strategy for risky people; a sponsor flag). Safety Nets become one-tap call or text in SOS. Stored locally; never upload contacts.

### Ex 4.5 — Practice Assertive Communication
- **Type:** repeatable form, per person: what I need from them; "I feel ___ when ___. I need ___."
- **App mapping:** a **script builder** with the formula template, Rick's example, a checklist of the assertive ingredients (appreciation, "I" statements, own concern, direct request, polite), and an optional AI or guided **role-play rehearsal** (see Plan).

### Ex 4.6 — Qualities of Safety Nets
- **Type:** multi-select plus "other."
- **Items:** not actively using; good listener; knows about addiction or is willing to learn; respectful; nonjudgmental; trustworthy; kind; nurturing; patient; makes time for me; not flaky; genuine; shares thoughts and ideas; protective of me and my recovery.
- **App mapping:** the chosen qualities become a rubric you can apply when tagging contacts in the network map.

### Ex 4.7 — Ways to Expand Your Network
- **Type:** three free-text lists:
  1. At least 2 ways to reach out to old or new friends (volunteering, a class, contacting old friends).
  2. At least 2 ways to join a community organization (AA/NA, religious community, a club, a counselor).
  3. At least 1 way to deepen a family relationship.
- **App mapping:** each item becomes an **action** in the Actions tracker (a "Build your crew" quest line).

## Non-exercise tools

- **SID decision helper:** the 5-step checklist plus an exit-plan prompt. Good as a quick tool ("Should I go?").
- **Refusal skills script:** a practice card and role-play.
- **Mutual-help meeting guide:** a meeting log (date, type, takeaway, "did I share or take a commitment?").

## App hooks

- This chapter is mostly **setup quests** (one-time and short-term actions) plus **trackers** (exercise, meetings).
- "Be Smart, Not Strong" is a good badge or slogan. Never design challenges that dare the user to resist exposure to triggers.
- Social features must be **opt-in** and privacy-first. The support map is the backbone of the SOS "call someone" action.
