# 00 — Front matter, Introduction, How to Use

Source: epub `index_split_001`–`005`. Paraphrased notes, not the book's text.

## Book at a glance

- **Title:** *The Addiction Recovery Skills Workbook: Changing Addictive Behaviors Using CBT, Mindfulness, and Motivational Interviewing Techniques*
- **Author:** Suzette Glasner-Edwards, PhD (UCLA Integrated Substance Abuse Programs). **Foreword:** Richard A. Rawson, PhD.
- **Publisher:** New Harbinger Publications. Copyrighted. See `README.md` for what that means for the app.
- **Audience:** adults with alcohol or drug addiction (diagnosed or not) who are open to an **abstinence-based** recovery. Behavioral addictions such as gambling are explicitly out of scope.
- **Positioning:** a self-help companion that works alone, alongside therapy, or alongside 12-step and other mutual-help groups. It is not a substitute for professional treatment.

## Structure

- **Part 1: Understanding Addictive Behaviors.** Ch 1 (how addiction happens) and Ch 2 (addiction as a brain disease). Psychoeducation plus self-assessment.
- **Part 2: Overcoming Addictive Behaviors.** Seven numbered "Steps" (Ch 3–9), then Ch 10, the personalized relapse prevention plan that pulls everything together.
  - Step 1 (Ch 3): Motivation and commitment (MI / stages of change)
  - Step 2 (Ch 4): Set yourself up to succeed (environment, decisions, exercise, social support, assertiveness)
  - Step 3 (Ch 5): Become a "self expert" (triggers, thoughts, distortions, cravings, CBT self-monitoring)
  - Step 4 (Ch 6): Respond to distortions (thought challenging, urge surfing, distract, delay, schedule, high-risk planning)
  - Step 5 (Ch 7): Become mindful (presence, breath, acceptance, compassion)
  - Step 6 (Ch 8): Rediscover rewards (behavioral activation, pleasant activities)
  - Step 7 (Ch 9): Conquer challenging emotions (mood, depression, anxiety, anger)
  - Ch 10: Personalized relapse prevention plan
- Back matter: Resources (drug facts, treatment locators, harm-reduction pointers), References, author bio.

## Three therapy approaches, one toolkit

1. **Motivational techniques (MI):** resolve ambivalence and commit. Revisit them any time motivation dips. The book says you can return to Ch 3 whenever you need to.
2. **CBT / relapse prevention:** addictive behavior is learned and conditioned to people, places, things and emotions. Identify triggers and learn new coping responses.
3. **Mindfulness (MBRP):** stay present using the breath, and accept discomfort (cravings, emotions) without acting on it or judging yourself.

## Core framing worth keeping in the app

- **Driving metaphor:** addiction breaks the brain's brakes, so it gets stuck in "go mode." Recovery means re-engaging "stop."
- **Addicted brain vs. rational brain:** two voices that recur through the whole book. The addicted brain tempts and drives you toward impulses. The rational brain (frontal lobe) weighs pros and cons and can be trained to get stronger. *This is the strongest narrative and character hook for gamification.*
- **Urges are normal.** Urges can last well into recovery and are **not a sign of failure**. They get less frequent and less intense over time, and skills make them manageable.
- **Relapse is common.** People often go through treatment, or relapse, several times before recovery sticks. "Keep at it and never give up."
- **No one-size-fits-all.** Try every skill in order, then judge which ones help *you*. The result is your own set of "go-to" skills. (That supports a "skill deck / loadout" mechanic.)
- **12-step compatible.** The skills fit with AA/NA and others, and meetings help with the social-support skills. Meetings aren't required.

## Stated goals of working through the book

1. Understand how addiction affected you psychologically and biologically, and what helps you heal.
2. Abstain from alcohol and drugs.
3. Check in on your motivation regularly and strengthen it when needed.
4. Learn healthy cognitive and behavioral coping skills.
5. Build a daily mindfulness practice for emotions and urges.
6. Know which treatments (psychological and medical) are available.

## Usage guidance from the book (useful for app flow)

- Do the Part 2 chapters **in order**, but **jump back to Ch 3 (motivation) any time**.
- Repeat each exercise **as many times as needed** to get comfortable. Exercises are meant to be practiced again and again, not done once.
- A therapist can use the exercises between sessions. This points to a possible "share with my therapist" export.

## App hooks

- Onboarding can mirror "Who this book can help": an abstinence-goal framing, a "not a replacement for treatment" notice, and an option to say you're working with a therapist or group.
- A persistent "Motivation" shortcut, because the book says to revisit Ch 3 at any time.
- Track which skills the user rates as helpful. That becomes their personal "go-to" toolkit, which feeds Ch 10's plan.
